A great biryani begins with the right grain. In Malabar and across southern India, festive kitchens reach not for long basmati, but for Kaima (also known as Jeerakasala), a small, short-grained, highly aromatic white rice.

When cooked gently with whole spices and ghee, Kaima grains remain light, individual, and release an unmistakable perfume that defines Thalassery and Malabar biryanis.

## The Character of the Grain

Unlike long-grain rice varieties that demand careful handling to prevent breakage, Kaima is resilient and absorbs meat and poultry stocks deeply without turning mushy.

## Ingredients for Kaima Dum Biryani

- 500g Kingsway Kaima / Jeerakasala Rice
- 750ml light bone stock or warm water
- 3 tbsp pure desi ghee
- 1 cinnamon stick, 4 green cardamom pods, 4 cloves, 1 bay leaf
- Fried shallots, roasted cashews, and golden raisins for garnish

## Preparation

Rinse the grains gently until the water runs clear. Soak for just 15 minutes before draining thoroughly. Sauté in warm ghee with whole aromatics before simmering until the liquid is absorbed. Rest under a tight lid for 10 minutes before gently fluffing with a fork.
